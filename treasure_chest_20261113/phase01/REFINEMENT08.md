Repeated reviews 83, 86 and 87 still reject the ring crease and lock slab depth. Stop the current groove approach rather than another radius tuning pass. The flat untextured renders and source show the causes: changing the entire tube radius at each groove changes its contour, and the assembled lock has 0.235 of exposed depth. The reference calls for fine divisions and much finer stepped plates.

1. Ring construction. Keep a closed constant major radius torus and one smoothly broadened lower collar. Machine three shallow divisions only into its broad cross section faces: let u run around the ring and v around the tube. Set tube radius rt(u,v) = collar_radius(u) minus groove_depth(u) times sin(v)^4. The weight and its first derivative are exactly zero at v=0 and v=pi, so the inner and outer ring contours stay unbroken circles. The grooves remain real shallow geometry on both broad faces, visible from either end. Use 128 uniform major samples plus 17 adaptive samples around each groove over plus or minus 0.060 radians, with Gaussian sigma 0.020 and depth 0.003. Use exact normals from cross derivatives in u and v, including both radius partial derivatives. Each ring remains one welded closed mesh, with no sleeve pieces or seam at the bottom. The groove locations are 2.52, pi and 3.76. This intentionally favors a clean contour over full circumference cuts; the mockup does not constrain the hidden side of each groove.

2. Lock construction. Reduce the depth coordinate of the lock backing, border and front face by half about local Z 1.88. Apply this before the existing one time height correction, and translate the associated fastening heads with their layer without compressing the dome geometry. Transform the plate normals by the appropriate inverse transpose. Reduce the front border inset from 0.095 to 0.060, about one small rivet radius, retaining explicit bevel geometry and the separate backing. Change the face outline inset from 0.089 to 0.054, preserving its existing 0.006 overlap beneath the border. Inspect the complete perimeter for exposed backing or a dark seam. Keep all true bores aligned. Move the lower latch roll and end in Z so its back remains seated against the new receiving face; alter the lower control points smoothly, keeping the upper pivot fixed. Do not squash unrelated body parts or the full hinge.

3. Barrel separation. Use a 0.72 main cylinder and two narrow 0.024 collar rings at each end, centers at plus or minus 0.28 and 0.335. Raised radii stay about 0.18 against the 0.168 main barrel. Move the small domed end pivots to plus or minus 0.365. The strip remains 0.51 wide, leaving the collars visible beside its edges. Retain the broad clipped mounting plate and its separate fixing heads.

4. Foot lower corners. Retain the clean four corner wrapped section. Clip only the four bottom vertices by about 0.075 with a single planar vertex bevel, then apply the normal rounded edge bevel. This places the clipping in the lowest tenth of the boot without restoring a wide vertical diagonal strip. Keep the existing controlled lower flare and broad front/end planes. Verify the complete bottom remains closed and its lowest height remains negative 2.455.

5. Washer rims. Reduce the seat radius from 1.12 times its head radius to 1.06 and its depth from 0.018 to 0.009, retaining controlled overlap under the dome and a small real edge bevel. Do not flatten the actual heads.
