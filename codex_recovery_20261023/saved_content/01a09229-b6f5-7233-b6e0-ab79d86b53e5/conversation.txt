Your proposed options menu makes sense as the entrance to the Blocks tab. I would put it there because that is where someone goes to explore or manage their protection.

The reason I favor this arrangement is that the actions have different timing:

- **Block now serves an immediate intention.** Someone has decided, “I need to put my phone down.” An extra choice interrupts that intention every time they use it.
- **Schedules and limits serve a recurring need.** Making them easy to discover matters because they can keep helping without another daily decision.
- **Badges make progress visible.** They give someone a reason to feel good about continuing, but that progress depends on getting protection running.

Your onboarding already creates a first schedule. That changes the tradeoff: new users have already encountered scheduling before reaching Home. Their next visit should make it clear that their setup is working and show what they have achieved.

The shortcut’s advantage is that people can immediately see where to start, without searching or scrolling. The general menu introduces another choice.

For retention, I’d favor the compact shortcut because it supports both immediate action and automatic routines. But the permanent space it takes on idle Home is a real cost, especially with your large badge card. There is no evidence yet that this tradeoff improves BestSpent’s retention.

**I’d prototype the Home button first and keep the Blocks changes modest.** Check that the badge, reclaimed-time number, and action fit comfortably on your iPhone 13 mini before committing to the larger redesign.
