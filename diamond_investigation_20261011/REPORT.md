Diamond appearance investigation, 2026-10-11

Scope: the last full-volume round Diamond, candidate 04, not the shallow installed badge. The candidate mobile USDZ has SHA-256 2ba20a7cf8a5efb7bcbe764879bc8205a9845f38f7c080aa1999a0d2c1ca3b16. No model or app source changes were made.

Finding: the strongest explanation for its flat appearance during the turn is the material, specifically a front-view optical picture carried mostly by emission. It is a genuinely 3D gem, but its apparent glass highlights and internal facets are mostly painted into that picture instead of being produced by changing reflections and transmission.

Evidence from the actual packaged round model:

- Its width and depth are both 6.24 units, with 240 triangles and a closed surface. Every mesh edge belongs to two triangles. All three corner normals agree within each triangle, and the largest difference between supplied normals and the real geometric face normal is 0.016 degrees. This excludes a thin model and broken or smoothed facet normals as the explanation for this version.
- Every one of its 240 triangles uses DiamondCrystal_Front, including its rear and sides. That material connects the color image to diffuseColor and the separate optical image to emissiveColor, with an emission multiplier of 4.0.
- The color image never exceeds 0.00973 in any linear-light channel. The emission image reaches 3.3875 after its multiplier. These are shader input ranges, not a measurement of the percentage of every rendered pixel supplied by reflection.
- The original optical_surface.py deliberately scaled the calibrated reflected component by 0.01 and placed the remaining target appearance in emission to remove colored studio reflection artifacts. The full-volume version reprojected those same maps around the new geometry. It therefore retained the material compromise that made the original front attractive.
- Both round textures repeat exactly every 256 columns of their 1024-pixel angular chart, corresponding to a quarter turn. The blue optical pattern and white glints are therefore repeated four times around the gem. As it turns, they remain attached to the surface and repeat, rather than becoming different reflections when each facet meets a light. The largely circular shape also keeps a similar silhouette, leaving changing material response especially important as a depth cue.
- The shader has metallic = 1 and roughness = 0.32. Opacity is not authored, so UsdPreviewSurface's opaque default of 1 applies. No transparent/refractive surface or clearcoat is configured. Its appearance is an opaque emissive metallic surface, rather than simulated glass transmission, internal reflections or diamond refraction.

It is not completely unlit: the nonzero color texture retains a small real reflected component. The important problem is that this component is too weak to drive most of the apparent crystal highlights. The front-view picture carries most of that visual structure. The app's existing studio environment and eight point lights are present, fixed independently of the rotating model; app loading does not replace the Diamond material. Normal maps are absent. The renderer applies AgX and glow after shading, and blurs the floor reflection separately; no Diamond-specific material override or surface blur was found. Shrinking during the Home turn reduces detail further, but does not explain the fixed optical pattern at full size.

The diagnosis is supported by packaged shader inputs, texture statistics, the construction scripts and the recorded app turn. No material ablation was performed because the owner asked for investigation only. The exact contribution of each cause to perceived flatness has not been isolated with changed-material tests.

A later material test should retain the full-volume geometry, blue identity and desired front composition, but let the actual facets produce a meaningful changing reflection response. The painted emission should supply restrained color or accents rather than most of the bright reflected structure. The opaque metal setup also needs evaluation against an appropriate non-metal crystal surface; transparency alone would not reconstruct diamond optics. No proposed fix was applied.
